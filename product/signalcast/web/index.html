01 · European Commission

Commission fines AdTech Ltd £4.2m over consent flows

The regulator fined AdTech Ltd for processing personal data without a lawful basis, specifically over pre-ticked consent in its onboarding. Firms running comparable flows have until 1 October to review them.

Why it matters: anyone running consent-based targeting in the EU now has a dated deadline and a worked example of what fails.

02 · NIST

Draft framework opens for public comment

NIST published a draft profile for generative AI systems and opened a comment period closing 15 September. The draft introduces documentation expectations that go beyond the existing RMF.

Why it matters: enterprise buyers cite NIST profiles in security questionnaires, so this becomes a procurement question within two quarters.

How it works

  • Five official sources are checked every week — regulators and standards bodies, not blogs.
  • Everything new is triaged against one question: does this change what a product team must do?
  • What survives gets a two-sentence summary and a plain statement of who it lands on.
  • Most weeks that is five to ten items. Some weeks it is three. You get the short issue rather than a padded one.

Triage is done by a language model working to a fixed editorial rubric, and summaries are drawn only from the source text. It is a filter, not a substitute for legal advice.
